Architecture Blueprint

How a Local AI Companion Works

The complete 5-stage loop from microphone ear to servo motion.

🎙️

Stage 1: Hearing & Perception (Audio Stream)

The companion listens via a digital I2S microphone (like the INMP441 or ReSpeaker HAT). A lightweight local wake-word listener (Vosk) runs continuously on low CPU/RAM. When triggered, the audio buffer is packaged into a PCM audio stream.

👂

Stage 2: Voice Transcription (Speech-To-Text)

The speech buffer is fed directly into an offline speech-to-text engine (faster-whisper or whisper.cpp). Running CTranslate2 quantization converts human speech into plain text tokens with sub-second latency.

🧠

Stage 3: LLM Brain, Tool Calling & Memory

The transcribed text hits an offline LLM inference daemon (Ollama, llama.cpp, or vLLM). Stateful memory agents (Letta/MemGPT) store conversational history. If local hardware bottlenecks, requests fall back seamlessly to on-demand cloud GPUs (RunPod).

🗣️

Stage 4: Voice Synthesis (Text-To-Speech)

The model's text response streams token-by-token into a local TTS engine (Piper or Kokoro-82M) or cloud voice API (ElevenLabs), converting words into natural audio without waiting for the full sentence to finish.

🤖

Stage 5: Physical Embodiment & Actuation

While speaking, the microcontroller (ESP32-S3 or Raspberry Pi) routes I2C commands to the PCA9685 driver to nod and turn head servos, animates expressive eyes on a round GC9A01 LCD screen, and flashes WS2812B RGB LEDs to reflect companion emotions.
