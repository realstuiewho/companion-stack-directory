🐳 What is "Docker Desktop" and why do I need it? ▼

Think of Docker like digital shipping containers.

Normally, installing AI models requires setting up dozens of confusing background tools, code libraries, and Python versions that can mess up your regular computer files.

Docker keeps everything neatly sealed inside its own clean sandbox. It downloads the exact tools you picked, connects them together automatically, and can be deleted cleanly in one click without leaving junk on your hard drive.

📄 What is "docker-compose.yml"? (The Blueprint) ▼

This is a plain-text instruction recipe for your computer.

When Docker reads this file, it sees: "Fetch the brain tool, open port 8283 for memory, give it a private storage folder, and hook up the voice engine."

Instead of typing 20 different setup commands into a terminal, this single recipe card tells your computer everything it needs to know in one breath.

🔑 What is "env.example"? (The Secret Key Safe) ▼

"ENV" stands for Environment. It’s where you safely store private account keys or passwords.

If you ever use cloud services like ElevenLabs (for realistic voices) or RunPod (for rented cloud graphics cards), you paste your secret keys into this file.

The blueprint reads your keys from here quietly so you never have to paste private passwords into your main software code!

🤖 How to Build Your First Desk Companion

The complete, no-jargon blueprint to bringing an AI brain into the physical world.

01. The Anatomy of a Deskbot ▼

Every companion robot on this directory breaks down into five distinct sensory layers:

  • 🧠 The Brain: The LLM (Large Language Model) that thinks, writes answers, and decides actions (e.g. Ollama or Groq).
  • 👂 The Ears: The microphone and speech-to-text engine that turns spoken sound waves into written sentences (e.g. INMP441 + faster-whisper).
  • 🗣️ The Voice: The text-to-speech engine and speaker amplifier that turns text into lifelike audio (e.g. Piper or ElevenLabs).
  • 👁️ The Eyes & Face: Circular LCD displays or camera modules that show blinking animated expressions and track your face.
  • 🤖 The Body: Micro servos and brackets that tilt, nod, and physically orient the companion toward you.
02. Choosing Your Compute: Cloud vs. Local PC ▼

Option A: Pure Cloud (Fastest & Lightest Hardware)

You use an inexpensive $7 ESP32-S3 microcontroller on your desk. When you speak, it streams audio over your home Wi-Fi to Groq and ElevenLabs. Total hardware: under $25 AUD.

Option B: 100% Sovereign Local (Private & Offline)

You run a dedicated Mini PC (like an AMD Ryzen with 32GB RAM or an Apple Silicon Mac) on your desk. Ollama, Whisper, and Piper run completely offline. Zero subscriptions, zero internet required.

03. The Audio Loop: Why I2S Beats Analog ▼

The #1 mistake beginners make is using cheap 3.5mm analog microphone jacks plugged into hobby boards. Microcontrollers emit electrical noise that sounds like a loud frying pan to AI speech recognizers.

Always choose I2S (Inter-IC Sound) digital modules like the INMP441 or MS4030. They convert sound directly into 24-bit binary data right on the silicon chip, giving the speech model pristine, noise-free audio every single time.

04. Physical Motion: Servos & Head Tilts ▼

To give your companion lifelike body language, you only need two motors mounted in a Pan/Tilt Gimbal Bracket (~$5):

  • Horizontal Servo: Turns the head left and right to look at you.
  • Vertical Servo: Nods the head up and down when agreeing or acknowledging commands.

Use metal gear servos like the MG90S rather than plastic nylon gears so your companion's neck never strips when moving heavy screens or camera brackets.

05. Giving It Memory & Character ▼

A smart model isn't fun if it sounds like a robotic customer service bot. You can give your companion a unique identity using two tools:

  • System Prompt: Tell the AI: "You are a witty, concise desk companion. Keep answers to 1-2 spoken sentences. Include motion tags like [NOD] or [TILT] when appropriate."
  • Letta Memory Layer: Connects a lifelong database so your bot remembers your name, projects, and past conversations across reboots.
06. The Top 3 Workbench Traps (Avoid These!) ▼

⚠️ Trap 1: Powering motors directly from microcontroller pins

Servos pull huge sudden spikes of current. If you wire them directly to the 5V pin of your ESP32 or Pi, the board will brown-out and reboot constantly. Always use a separate 5V power wire or capacitor.

⚠️ Trap 2: Charge-only USB cables

Many cheap phone charging cables omit the data wires. If your computer doesn't detect your board when plugged in, switch to a verified USB-C 4-wire data cable.

⚠️ Trap 3: Missing Common Ground

If using a separate battery for your motors, all GND (ground) pins between your controller and battery must be tied together, otherwise signal voltages float and servos will twitch violently.
